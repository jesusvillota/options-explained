## <span class="section-tag">Hook</span>One integral away

We've assembled every ingredient:

- **Chapter 12:** price = discounted expected payoff under the risk-neutral probability $\mathbb{Q}$.
- **Chapters 14 and 16:** under $\mathbb{Q}$, the stock at expiry is lognormal,
  $\Spot{S_T} = \Spot{S}\exp\big((\Rate{r} - \tfrac12\Vol{\sigma}^2)\Time{T} + \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}\,Z\big)$ with $Z$ standard normal.

So the price of a call is one integral:

$$
\Call{C} = e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}\;\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[(\Spot{S_T} - \Strike{K})^+\big].
$$

In this chapter we compute it, and get the formula that earned Scholes and Merton the 1997 Nobel prize:

$$
\boxed{\;\Call{C} = \Spot{S}\,N(d_1) - \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}\,N(d_2)\;}
\qquad d_{1,2} = \frac{\ln(\Spot{S}/\Strike{K}) + (\Rate{r} \pm \tfrac12\Vol{\sigma}^2)\Time{T}}{\Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}}.
$$

More importantly, we'll see what each piece *means*. It's two simple bets, not one opaque formula.

## <span class="section-tag">Intuition</span>Split the payoff in two

At expiry, if the call finishes in the money you receive the stock and pay the strike. If not, nothing happens.
So the payoff is the difference of two simpler contracts:

$$
(\Spot{S_T} - \Strike{K})^+ \;=\; \underbrace{\Spot{S_T}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K}\}}_{\text{get the stock if ITM}} \;-\; \underbrace{\Strike{K}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K}\}}_{\text{pay the strike if ITM}} .
$$

**The cash leg.** Paying $\Strike{K}$ "if in the money" is worth $\Strike{K}$, discounted, times the
risk-neutral probability of finishing in the money. That probability is $N(d_2)$, so the cash leg is worth
$\Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}N(d_2)$.

**The stock leg.** Receiving the stock "if in the money" is worth $\Spot{S}N(d_1)$. It's tempting to read
$N(d_1)$ as the same probability again, but it's larger. The stock tends to be in the money exactly in the
scenarios where it's worth a lot, and that correlation pushes the value up. $N(d_1)$ is the probability of
finishing in the money **measured in units of the stock**, a different "risk-neutral" world where the stock, not
cash, is the numeraire.

<Callout type="key" title="Reading the formula">
$\Call{C} = \Spot{S}\,N(d_1) - \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}N(d_2)$ says: <em>receive the stock if in the money,
pay the strike if in the money</em>. $N(d_2)$ is the risk-neutral chance of exercise. $N(d_1)$ is the same event
seen from the stock's point of view, and (Chapter 21) it's also the call's <strong>delta</strong>, the number of
shares in the replicating portfolio.
</Callout>

## <span class="section-tag">Intuition</span>Sanity checks

A formula you can't sanity-check is a formula you can't trust. Each limit below matches something we already know:

- **Deep in the money** ($\Spot{S} \gg \Strike{K}$): $d_1, d_2 \to \infty$, both $N$'s → 1, and
  $\Call{C} \to \Spot{S} - \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}$, the forward's value. That's the lower bound from
  Chapter 7.
- **Deep out of the money**: both $N$'s → 0, so $\Call{C} \to 0$.
- **No volatility** ($\Vol{\sigma} \to 0$): the future is certain, $\Spot{S_T} = \Spot{S}e^{\Rate{r}\Time{T}}$, and
  $\Call{C} = (\Spot{S} - \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}})^+$.
- **Huge volatility** ($\Vol{\sigma} \to \infty$): $d_1 \to \infty$, $d_2 \to -\infty$, so $\Call{C} \to \Spot{S}$,
  the upper bound from Chapter 7.

**Puts** follow from put–call parity (Chapter 8):

$$
\Put{P} = \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}\,N(-d_2) - \Spot{S}\,N(-d_1).
$$

## <span class="section-tag">Formalisation</span>The derivation

Write $\Spot{S_T} = \Spot{S}e^{(\Rate{r} - \frac12\Vol{\sigma}^2)\Time{T} + \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}Z}$ with $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ under
$\mathbb{Q}$. The call is in the money when

$$
\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K} \iff Z > -d_2, \qquad d_2 = \frac{\ln(\Spot{S}/\Strike{K}) + (\Rate{r} - \tfrac12\Vol{\sigma}^2)\Time{T}}{\Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}}.
$$

**Cash leg.** $\mathbb{Q}(\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K}) = \mathbb{Q}(Z > -d_2) = N(d_2)$, so
$e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\Strike{K}\mathbf{1}\{\Spot{S_T}>\Strike{K}\}] = \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}N(d_2)$.

**Stock leg.** Write out the expectation and complete the square in the exponent:

$$
e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\Spot{S_T}\mathbf{1}\{Z > -d_2\}\big]
= \Spot{S}\int_{-d_2}^{\infty} e^{-\frac12\Vol{\sigma}^2\Time{T} + \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}z}\,\frac{e^{-z^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,dz
= \Spot{S}\int_{-d_2}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(z - \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}})^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,dz .
$$

Substitute $y = z - \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}$. The lower limit becomes $-d_2 - \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}} = -d_1$, so the
integral is $N(d_1)$ with $d_1 = d_2 + \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}$. Subtract the two legs and you have the formula.

**The share measure.** The shift $z \mapsto z - \Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}$ is a *change of probability*. Weighting each
scenario by $\Spot{S_T}/(\Spot{S}e^{\Rate{r}\Time{T}})$ moves the mean of $Z$ up by $\Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}$, and under the new
probability $\mathbb{Q}^S$ the event $\{\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K}\}$ has probability $N(d_1)$. So

$$
\Call{C} = \Spot{S}\;\mathbb{Q}^{S}(\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K}) \;-\; \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}\;\mathbb{Q}(\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K}).
$$

This "change of numeraire" is the gateway to Part X.

**With dividends** (yield $q$), replace $\Spot{S}$ by $\Spot{S}e^{-q\Time{T}}$ and $\Rate{r}$ by $\Rate{r} - q$ in the drift:
$\Call{C} = \Spot{S}e^{-q\Time{T}}N(d_1) - \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}N(d_2)$ with
$d_{1,2} = \big[\ln(\Spot{S}/\Strike{K}) + (\Rate{r} - q \pm \tfrac12\Vol{\sigma}^2)\Time{T}\big]/(\Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}})$.
This is Merton's version. With $q$ as the foreign rate it prices currency options (Garman–Kohlhagen).

## <span class="section-tag">Recap</span>What to remember

- $\Call{C} = \Spot{S}N(d_1) - \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}N(d_2)$: **receive the stock if ITM** minus **pay the strike if
  ITM**.
- $N(d_2) = \mathbb{Q}(\Spot{S_T} > \Strike{K})$. $N(d_1)$ is the same event under the **share measure**, and it's
  the call's delta.
- The derivation is one Gaussian integral with a completed square. The shift by $\Vol{\sigma}\sqrt{\Time{T}}$ is a change of
  probability.
- Puts by parity: $\Put{P} = \Strike{K}e^{-\Rate{r}\Time{T}}N(-d_2) - \Spot{S}N(-d_1)$. Dividends: replace $\Spot{S}$ with
  $\Spot{S}e^{-q\Time{T}}$.

**Next up:** Black and Scholes didn't get their formula from an expectation. They got it from a **hedging
argument** that produces a partial differential equation. Chapter 19 follows their route, and finds the heat
equation hiding inside.
