## <span class="section-tag">Hook</span>Betting on volatility, and only volatility

Suppose you think the market will be turbulent over the next year, but you have no idea in which direction. Buying
an at-the-money straddle is a start, but it's a messy bet. Its sensitivity to volatility depends on where the stock
goes: once the stock moves away from the strike, gamma fades (Chapter 21) and so does your exposure. Delta-hedging
it, as in Chapter 24, leaves you with a P&L weighted by $\Gamma\Spot{S}^2$ along the path.

A **variance swap** is the clean version. At expiry it pays the difference between the variance the stock actually
realised and a strike agreed today:

$$
\text{payoff} = N_{\text{var}}\big(\Vol{\sigma}_R^2 - K_{\text{var}}\big).
$$

What's the fair strike? Remarkably, it can be read off today's option prices, with no model for volatility at all.
And the recipe, applied to S&P 500 options, is exactly how the **VIX** index is computed.

## <span class="section-tag">Intuition</span>The log contract

Here's the key fact, from Itô's lemma (Chapter 17). For any price path,

$$
\ln\frac{\Spot{S_T}}{\Spot{S_0}} = \int_0^{\Time{T}}\frac{d\Spot{S_t}}{\Spot{S_t}} - \frac12\int_0^{\Time{T}}\Vol{\sigma}_t^2\,dt .
$$

The total variance is twice the gap between "the sum of returns" and "the log of the total return". The first can
be earned by holding a fixed *dollar* amount of stock, rebalanced continuously: that's $1/\Spot{S}$ shares. The
second is the payoff of a **log contract**, which pays $\ln \Spot{S_T}$. So variance equals a dynamic stock position
minus two log contracts, whatever volatility does along the way.

Nobody trades log contracts. But Chapter 27 showed that any payoff can be built from options weighted by its
curvature. The curvature of $-\ln \Strike{K}$ is $1/\Strike{K}^2$:

<Callout type="key" title="The fear gauge measures the price of variance">
The VIX is often called the "fear index". More precisely, it's the market price of 30-day variance: what you'd
pay today, in volatility units, for the realised variance of the next month. Like implied volatility (Chapter 25),
it usually sits a few points above the volatility that's later realised. That gap is the variance risk premium,
which sellers of variance swaps earn in calm times and pay back in crashes.
</Callout>

## <span class="section-tag">Formalisation</span>Replicating variance

Assume the stock follows $d\Spot{S_t} = \mu_t\Spot{S_t}\,dt + \Vol{\sigma}_t\Spot{S_t}\,dW_t$, where the volatility
$\Vol{\sigma}_t$ can be any random process, but there are no jumps. Itô's lemma for $\ln \Spot{S_t}$ gives

$$
\frac{1}{\Time{T}}\int_0^{\Time{T}}\Vol{\sigma}_t^2\,dt = \frac{2}{\Time{T}}\left[\int_0^{\Time{T}}\frac{d\Spot{S_t}}{\Spot{S_t}} - \ln\frac{\Spot{S_T}}{\Spot{S_0}}\right] .
$$

Take risk-neutral expectations. The stock position earns $\Rate{r}\Time{T}$, and with the forward
$F = \Spot{S_0}e^{\Rate{r}\Time{T}}$:

$$
K_{\text{var}} = \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\frac{1}{\Time{T}}\int_0^{\Time{T}}\Vol{\sigma}_t^2\,dt\Big]
= \frac{2}{\Time{T}}\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\frac{\Spot{S_T} - F}{F} - \ln\frac{\Spot{S_T}}{F}\Big] .
$$

The bracket is the convex payoff in the widget, zero at $F$ with zero slope there. Apply Carr–Madan (Chapter 27)
with $g(s) = (s - F)/F - \ln(s/F)$, so that $g(F) = g'(F) = 0$ and $g''(\Strike{K}) = 1/\Strike{K}^2$:

$$
\frac{\Spot{S_T} - F}{F} - \ln\frac{\Spot{S_T}}{F} = \int_0^F \frac{(\Strike{K} - \Spot{S_T})^+}{\Strike{K}^2}\,d\Strike{K} + \int_F^\infty \frac{(\Spot{S_T} - \Strike{K})^+}{\Strike{K}^2}\,d\Strike{K} .
$$

Pricing both sides,

$$
K_{\text{var}} = \frac{2e^{\Rate{r}\Time{T}}}{\Time{T}}\left[\int_0^F \frac{\Put{P}(\Strike{K})}{\Strike{K}^2}\,d\Strike{K} + \int_F^\infty \frac{\Call{C}(\Strike{K})}{\Strike{K}^2}\,d\Strike{K}\right] .
$$

With a flat smile at $\Vol{\sigma}$ this gives exactly $\Vol{\sigma}^2$. With a skew it's more, because the
far-out puts are priced at higher volatilities and weighted most.

**Why it's a pure volatility bet.** The log contract has $\Spot{S}^2\,\partial^2_{\Spot{S}}(-\ln \Spot{S}) = 1$: its
**dollar gamma is constant**. In Chapter 24, a delta-hedged option earned
$\tfrac12\Gamma\Spot{S}^2(\Vol{\sigma}_R^2 - \Vol{\sigma}_I^2)\,dt$ with a weight that depended on the path. The strip
makes that weight the same everywhere, so the P&L depends only on realised variance.

**The VIX formula.** Cboe computes, for each of two expiries around 30 days,

$$
\Vol{\sigma}^2 = \frac{2}{\Time{T}}\sum_i \frac{\Delta\Strike{K}_i}{\Strike{K}_i^2}\,e^{\Rate{r}\Time{T}}Q(\Strike{K}_i) - \frac{1}{\Time{T}}\Big(\frac{F}{\Strike{K}_0} - 1\Big)^2,
$$

where $Q$ is the out-of-the-money option's mid price, $\Strike{K}_0$ is the first listed strike at or below $F$
(where $Q$ averages the put and call), and $\Delta\Strike{K}_i$ is half the distance between neighbouring strikes.
The last term corrects for $\Strike{K}_0 \ne F$. It then interpolates the two variances to exactly 30 days and
reports $\text{VIX} = 100\sqrt{\Vol{\sigma}^2}$.

**Caveats.**
- **Volatility swaps** pay $\Vol{\sigma}_R - K_{\text{vol}}$. By Jensen's inequality, $K_{\text{vol}} < \sqrt{K_{\text{var}}}$,
  and the gap depends on the volatility of volatility, so a vol swap can't be replicated statically.
- **Jumps** break the identity. A jump adds to realised variance differently from how it moves the log contract,
  so the strip misprices variance when the stock can gap (Chapter 31).
- **Truncation.** Only a finite range of strikes trades, so real replication leaves out the far tails, as the
  widget's range slider shows.

## <span class="section-tag">Recap</span>What to remember

- Itô: realised variance = 2 × (dynamic $1/\Spot{S}$-shares position − log contract). With no jumps this holds
  whatever volatility does.
- The log contract is a strip of out-of-the-money options weighted by $1/\Strike{K}^2$, so
  $K_{\text{var}} = \frac{2e^{\Rate{r}\Time{T}}}{\Time{T}}\int Q(\Strike{K})/\Strike{K}^2\,d\Strike{K}$.
- The strip has constant dollar gamma: a pure bet on variance. With a skew, $\sqrt{K_{\text{var}}}$ exceeds
  at-the-money vol.
- The **VIX** is this formula on 30-day S&P 500 options: the price of next month's variance.

the simplest idea: let volatility depend on the stock price and time, and choose that function to fit the surface
