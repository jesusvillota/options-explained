## <span class="section-tag">Hook</span>A million shares by the close

You must sell 1 million shares of a \$100 stock before the market closes, about a tenth of the day's volume. Sell
them all in the opening minutes and you walk deep into the book: Chapter 61 says you pay dearly for speed. Spread
them evenly over the day and impact is small, but you hold a \$100 million position for hours, and a 2% move against
you costs \$2 million.

There is no free choice: speed costs impact, patience costs risk. In 2000, Robert Almgren and Neil Chriss turned this
dilemma into a clean optimisation problem and solved it in closed form. Their answer, and the efficient frontier it
traces, sits behind the *implementation shortfall* algorithms that banks and brokers have offered to clients ever
since. It's also the problem a dealer faces when a large option trade leaves it with a big delta to hedge.

## <span class="section-tag">Intuition</span>Sell fast while you hold the most risk

Two forces pull in opposite directions.

- **Impact** depends on how fast you trade. Selling twice as fast costs twice as much per share, so you'd like to
  spread the sale evenly over all the time you have. That's **TWAP**: sell at a constant rate.
- **Risk** depends on how much you still hold. Every share you haven't sold yet is exposed to the price moving. So
  you'd like to get rid of the position quickly.

The compromise is to sell **faster at the start**, when the position and its risk are largest, and slow down as the
position shrinks. How front-loaded the schedule is depends on one number, the **urgency** $\kappa$: high when you're
risk averse or the stock is volatile, low when impact is expensive.

The position still to sell at time $t$ is $x_t = X\,\dfrac{\sinh\big(\kappa(T - t)\big)}{\sinh(\kappa T)}$, with urgency

## <span class="section-tag">Formalisation</span>Mean, variance and the calculus of variations

**The model.** You hold $x_t$ shares at time $t$, starting from $x_0 = X$ and finishing at $x_T = 0$, and you sell
at rate $v_t = -\dot x_t$. The price follows

$$
\Spot{S}_t = \Spot{S}_0 + \Vol{\sigma} W_t - \lambda\,(X - x_t),
$$

an arithmetic Brownian motion pushed down by **permanent impact** $\lambda$ for every share sold so far. Each trade
also pays **temporary impact**: you sell at $\tilde S_t = \Spot{S}_t - \eta\,v_t$, a discount proportional to your
speed, which doesn't persist. Both impacts are linear: Kyle's model for $\lambda$, and a book of constant depth for $\eta$.

**Implementation shortfall.** The cost of the sale is what you would have received at the starting price, minus what
you actually receive:

$$
C = X\Spot{S}_0 - \int_0^T \tilde S_t\,v_t\,dt .
$$

Substituting the prices and integrating by parts (the $W_t$ term becomes $\int_0^T x_t\,dW_t$, since $x_T = 0$ and
$W_0 = 0$) gives three pieces:

$$
C = \tfrac12\lambda X^2 + \eta\int_0^T v_t^2\,dt - \Vol{\sigma}\int_0^T x_t\,dW_t .
$$

The first is the permanent impact: whatever the schedule, your later shares are sold at prices depressed by your
earlier ones, and the total is always $\tfrac12\lambda X^2$. The second is the temporary impact. The third is the
price risk. For a schedule fixed in advance, the Itô isometry (Chapter 17) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[C] &= \tfrac12\lambda X^2 + \eta\int_0^T \dot x_t^2\,dt, \\
\mathrm{Var}[C] &= \Vol{\sigma}^2\int_0^T x_t^2\,dt .
\end{aligned}
$$

**The optimal schedule.** A trader with risk aversion $\gamma$ minimises $\mathbb{E}[C] + \gamma\,\mathrm{Var}[C]$.
The permanent term is a constant, so the problem is to minimise

$$
\int_0^T \Big(\eta\,\dot x_t^2 + \gamma\Vol{\sigma}^2 x_t^2\Big)\,dt .
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equation of the calculus of variations, $\frac{d}{dt}\,\partial_{\dot x}L = \partial_x L$, gives

$$
2\eta\,\ddot x_t = 2\gamma\Vol{\sigma}^2\,x_t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ddot x_t = \kappa^2 x_t ,
$$

with $\kappa^2 = \gamma\Vol{\sigma}^2/\eta$. The solutions are combinations of $e^{\kappa t}$ and $e^{-\kappa t}$, and
the one with $x_0 = X$ and $x_T = 0$ is

$$
x_t = X\,\frac{\sinh\big(\kappa(T - t)\big)}{\sinh(\kappa T)} .
$$

**Two limits.** As $\gamma \to 0$, $\kappa \to 0$ and $\sinh$ becomes linear: $x_t = X(1 - t/T)$, which is TWAP, with

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[C] &= \tfrac12\lambda X^2 + \frac{\eta X^2}{T}, \\
\mathrm{Var}[C] &= \frac{\Vol{\sigma}^2 X^2 T}{3} .
\end{aligned}
$$

When $\kappa T$ is large, $x_t \approx X e^{-\kappa t}$: the position decays exponentially, with **half-life**
$\ln 2/\kappa$, whatever the deadline. An urgent seller barely notices $T$.

**The efficient frontier.** Each $\gamma$ gives one point $(\mathrm{Var}[C], \mathbb{E}[C])$. Together they trace a
convex curve: no schedule has both lower expected cost and lower variance than a point on it. Since the optimum
minimises $\mathbb{E} + \gamma\,\mathrm{Var}$, the frontier's slope at that point is

$$
\frac{d\,\mathbb{E}[C]}{d\,\mathrm{Var}[C]} = -\gamma .
$$

At TWAP ($\gamma = 0$) the slope is zero: the first reduction in variance costs nothing to first order. Almgren and
Chriss stressed this point: a risk-neutral trader should still accept a little risk aversion. In the widget's example,
TWAP's expected cost is \$350,000 with a standard deviation of \$1.15 million. At $\gamma = 10^{-7}$ per dollar, the
expected cost rises by 2.7% and the standard deviation falls by almost 8%.

**What the model leaves out.** Impact is linear, while Chapter 61 found a square root; with power-law temporary
impact the problem has no closed form but is easy to solve numerically, and the trajectories look similar. Impact
here is either instantaneous or permanent; Chapter 63 adds impact that decays over time. And the schedule is fixed in
advance: under mean–variance, letting the schedule react to prices can do slightly better, as Almgren and Lorenz
showed in 2007, but the static solution remains the industry's reference point.

## <span class="section-tag">Recap</span>What to remember

- Trading fast costs **impact**; trading slowly costs **risk**. The **implementation shortfall** is
  $C = \tfrac12\lambda X^2 + \eta\int v^2\,dt - \sigma\int x\,dW$.
- Minimising $\mathbb{E}[C] + \gamma\,\mathrm{Var}[C]$ gives $\ddot x = \kappa^2 x$ and the trajectory
  $x_t = X\sinh(\kappa(T - t))/\sinh(\kappa T)$, with $\kappa = \sqrt{\gamma\sigma^2/\eta}$.
- $\gamma = 0$ is **TWAP**; large $\kappa T$ is exponential decay with half-life $\ln 2/\kappa$.
- The **efficient frontier** has slope $-\gamma$, flat at TWAP: a little risk reduction is free.
- Linear **permanent impact** doesn't change the optimal schedule.

**Next up:** impact in Almgren–Chriss is either instant or permanent. Real books refill after you trade, at a finite
speed. Chapter 63 models that resilience and finds that the best strategy trades a block at the start, steadily in
between, and a block at the end.
