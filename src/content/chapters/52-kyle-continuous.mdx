## <span class="section-tag">Hook</span>Don't show your hand at the open

The insider from Chapter 51 knows the call is worth 2.78, not 2.48, and the news will be public at tonight's close.
This time there's a whole trading day. Should it buy everything at the open?

If it does, the market maker sees a huge buy flow at 9:30, marks the price up, and the insider's edge is gone by
9:31. If instead it trades a little at a time, each small order hides among the day's noise trading, and the
insider can keep buying at prices that haven't caught up yet. But it mustn't be *too* slow: whatever is still
unexploited at the close is lost, because the news comes out anyway.

Kyle solved the many-round version in his 1985 paper, and Kerry Back gave the clean continuous-time version in 1992.
The insider's optimal strategy turns the price into an object we met in Chapter 15, a **Brownian bridge**, pinned at
today's price and at the insider's value tonight.

## <span class="section-tag">Intuition</span>Trading at the rate the market forgets

In continuous time, noise traders send a steady random stream of buys and sells. The insider trades at a **rate**:
the gap between its value and the current price, divided by the time left:

$$
\text{rate} \propto \frac{v - p_t}{T - t} .
$$

Early in the day the time left is long, so the insider trades gently even if the mispricing is large. As the close
approaches it trades harder and harder on whatever gap remains, and the gap is closed exactly at the bell.

From the market maker's point of view, nothing looks unusual. Total order flow looks like plain noise, the price moves
like a Brownian motion, and its price impact $\lambda$ is the same all day. It can't tell when the insider is
trading, because the insider trades exactly so as not to be told apart. Yet by the close, the price has learned
everything. From the insider's point of view the same price is a **bridge**: a random path with a drift that pulls it
towards $v$ ever more strongly as time runs out.

<Callout type="key" title="Information enters at a constant rate">
In continuous-time Kyle, the market's remaining uncertainty falls linearly, $\Sigma_t = \Sigma_0(1 - t/T)$; price impact
is constant, $\lambda = \sqrt{\Sigma_0}/(\sigma_u\sqrt{T})$; and the insider earns $\sigma_u\sqrt{\Sigma_0 T}$, twice what it
earns trading once. Spreading trades over time hides them better, and patience pays.
</Callout>

## <span class="section-tag">Formalisation</span>Back's equilibrium

**Setup.** The value $v \sim N(p_0, \Sigma_0)$ becomes public at time $T$. Noise traders' cumulative order is
$Z_t = \sigma_u W_t$, with $W$ a Brownian motion independent of $v$. The insider's cumulative order is
$X_t = \int_0^t \theta_s\,ds$, and the market maker sees the total flow $Y_t = X_t + Z_t$ and sets
$p_t = \mathbb{E}[v \mid Y_s, s \le t]$. We look for an equilibrium with a constant linear pricing rule
$dp_t = \lambda\,dY_t$.

**The insider is indifferent at every instant.** The insider maximises $\mathbb{E}\big[\int_0^T (v - p_t)\,\theta_t\,dt\big]$.
Guess the value function

$$
J(t, p) = \frac{(v - p)^2}{2\lambda} + \frac{\lambda\sigma_u^2}{2}(T - t) .
$$

Its HJB equation (Chapter 19's PDE methods, with a control) is
$\max_\theta \big\{(v - p)\,\theta + J_t + \lambda\theta\,J_p + \tfrac12\lambda^2\sigma_u^2 J_{pp}\big\} = 0$. With
$J_p = -(v - p)/\lambda$, $J_{pp} = 1/\lambda$ and $J_t = -\lambda\sigma_u^2/2$:

- the terms in $\theta$ are $(v - p)\theta - (v - p)\theta = 0$, whatever $\theta$ is;
- the rest is $-\tfrac12\lambda\sigma_u^2 + \tfrac12\lambda^2\sigma_u^2 \cdot \tfrac1\lambda = 0$.

So *every* trading rate is equally good at every instant. What matters is the end: the insider must not leave any
mispricing unexploited, which means it must make $p_T = v$. The rate

$$
\theta_t = \frac{v - p_t}{\lambda\,(T - t)}
$$

does exactly that, and its expected profit is $J(0, p_0) = (v - p_0)^2/(2\lambda) + \lambda\sigma_u^2 T/2$.

**The price is a Brownian bridge.** Substituting the insider's rate,

$$
\begin{aligned}
dp_t &= \lambda\,(\theta_t\,dt + \sigma_u\,dW_t) \\
&= \frac{v - p_t}{T - t}\,dt + \lambda\sigma_u\,dW_t ,
\end{aligned}
$$

which is the stochastic differential equation of a Brownian bridge from $p_0$ at time 0 to $v$ at time $T$, with
volatility $\lambda\sigma_u$.

**The market maker's filter pins down $\lambda$.** To the market maker, $dY_t = a_t\,(v - p_t)\,dt + \sigma_u\,dW_t$ with
$a_t = 1/(\lambda(T - t))$ and $v$ unknown. This is a linear Gaussian filtering problem, solved by the Kalman–Bucy
filter: the conditional mean moves by $dp_t = (\Sigma_t a_t/\sigma_u^2)\,dY_t$ and the conditional variance by
$d\Sigma_t/dt = -\Sigma_t^2 a_t^2/\sigma_u^2$. A constant $\lambda$ requires $\Sigma_t a_t/\sigma_u^2 = \lambda$, that is
$\Sigma_t = \lambda^2\sigma_u^2(T - t)$. Then $d\Sigma_t/dt = -\lambda^2\sigma_u^2$, consistent, and the initial condition
$\Sigma_0 = \lambda^2\sigma_u^2 T$ gives

$$
\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{\Sigma_0}}{\sigma_u\sqrt{T}}, \qquad\qquad \Sigma_t = \Sigma_0\Big(1 - \frac{t}{T}\Big) .
$$

The price's volatility in the market maker's eyes is $\lambda\sigma_u = \sqrt{\Sigma_0/T}$: the private information
gets released at a constant rate of $\Sigma_0/T$ per unit of time.

**Profits.** Averaging $J(0, p_0)$ over $v$, with $\mathbb{E}[(v - p_0)^2] = \Sigma_0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[\pi_{\text{insider}}] &= \frac{\Sigma_0}{2\lambda} + \frac{\lambda\sigma_u^2 T}{2} \\
&= \frac{\sigma_u\sqrt{\Sigma_0 T}}{2} + \frac{\sigma_u\sqrt{\Sigma_0 T}}{2} \\
&= \sigma_u\sqrt{\Sigma_0 T} .
\end{aligned}
$$

With $T = 1$ that is twice the one-shot profit $\sigma_u\sqrt{\Sigma_0}/2$ of Chapter 51, and it all comes from noise
traders. A deeper insight of Back's paper is that in equilibrium the total order flow $Y$, seen in its own filtration,
is a Brownian motion with volatility $\sigma_u$: statistically indistinguishable from noise alone.

**In the widget's units.** With $\sqrt{\Sigma_0}$ = 30 cents, $\sigma_u$ = 1,000 contracts per $\sqrt{\text{day}}$ and
$T$ = one day, $\lambda = 0.30/1000 = 0.0003$ dollars per contract (3 cents per 100 contracts), and the expected profit
is $1000 \times 0.30 = 300$ per share-unit, or \$30,000 in contract terms.

## <span class="section-tag">Recap</span>What to remember

- With time to trade, the insider trades at rate $\theta_t = (v - p_t)/(\lambda(T - t))$, hiding in the noise.
- The price impact is constant, $\lambda = \sqrt{\Sigma_0}/(\sigma_u\sqrt{T})$, and the market's uncertainty falls linearly,
  $\Sigma_t = \Sigma_0(1 - t/T)$.
- To the market maker the price is a Brownian motion; to the insider it's a **Brownian bridge** from $p_0$ to $v$.
- The insider earns $\sigma_u\sqrt{\Sigma_0 T}$, twice the one-shot profit, all paid by noise traders.
